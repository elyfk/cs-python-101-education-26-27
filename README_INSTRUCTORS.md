# Python 101 - METU IEEE CS

Welcome to the official repository for **Python 101**, hosted by the METU IEEE Computer Society (CS). This repository belongs to the 2026-2027 term. Starting from this term, we employed many changes to the obsolete CS Education System. Anteriorly, the content was stored in Google Drive as Google Colab sheets. It was long, spanning 8 weeks and 2 weeks dedicated to 2 specific libraries with no solid need. Therefore this term's revolutionary changes include:
1. All the education documents are re-wirtten and completely migrated to GitHub.
2. Further Reading parts are created for each week's content, which people really take a look at it.
3. Online editing and coding made possible via GitHub Codespaces.
4. The slides used within the introductory meeting has been made with the iconic library **manim**.
5. 1 week is cut short, though this was the planned one but we couldn't make it real, the details are below in the document.
6. 2 last weeks are dedicated to learning specific concepts/applications rather diving deep into specific and limited libraries. Also this was planned couldn't get into reality for the reasons elaborated below inside the document.
7. 2 `.bat` and 2 `.sh` files created in order to make students easily download Python to their computers without any friction. 

## Course Overview

This year's course overview. All the major changes have been listed above. Now there are the details.

* **Instructor(s):** [@taglukahmet](https://github.com/taglukahmet), the CS/AESS chair.
* **Duration:** 6 Weeks (4 + 2). This was acutally planned to be 5 + 2 weeks. But our first week has been cancelled since the introduction meeting's population has overwhelmed the auditorium, some stayed standing. Therefore we had to cram 5 weeks of content into 4 weeks since we avoided extra load. This of course increased each lectures' durations a bit, but it was bearable with 10 min breaks in the middle.
* **Prerequisites:** None required, this education is completely beginner-centred. This is the actual education for the members, which will later become active members when they pass the education. Alternatively, they can take the exemption exam to become active members.
* **Education Content:** The first 4 weeks are already included in the repository itself. For the 2 weeks, we had some problems. Due to midterms we weren't able to reserve a proper place to give more education. Therefore we decided to give them 2 weeks in order to make some mini-projects of their choice. They will fork the repo, and put their projects into the `assignments/` folder.
* **Homeworks:** We actually intended to do this, but GitHub classroom was retired at the end of the august to our surprise. Therefore we had to look for a solid solution, however unfortunately we didn't have enough time to engineer a proper solution, since we were working on the exemption exam.

## Advices to The Upcoming Generations and Instructors

Please take here seriously, GitHub is the place where small individuals make a difference and show to the whole work that they can change world. I am talking out of experience here. Listen carefully and you will surely benefit.

1. Reserving and selecting auditoriums are crucial. Always opt for something big. Computer engineering auditoriums are always beneficial. Especially try to reserve BMB-1 as early as possible for the introduction meeting. 100+ people really come to that very first introduction. They won't fit to other BMB's and D131 as that crowd. For visibility, you can also select BMB1 later too, I did and it worked. Because messaging that you're not limiting yourself by the EEE Department is key to success on reaching a diverse and crowded group. We thankfully managed it through, did most of the classes at BMB1, and became even more populous than the CENG originated societies.
2. Do your study, do your research before each class. Master each of your weak points on Python. They did to me and will ask you crazy questions. Some might be about even how a Programming Language work in general. Get ready, I was ready and I managed it through, be the same. Whenever you answer a question properly, it's a huge plus. They always want to continue with a guy who really knows stuff. You can live demonstrate everything when they ask. You can run a code and show it fails just to prove a point. Especially on lists, try to show it as much as you can. It took one hour for me, just because of the never ending questions.
3. Be friends with the class, with the people. Always smile, always make them smile. Make a little pun, explain it with a joke, or make the examples fun. All of them pays off in time. They get more attached and follow the course. We started the year with around 80 people, and at the last class we had about 40. We started just a little bit lower than the previous years but ended with more than ever, maybe double the past year. Interacting with them matters. Interact at every chance. Make them code something little. Even a simple loop. After the week 2, you will sure be able to do that every time.
4. Make the education 5 + 2 again. That's way better. We had problems, but I feel that you won't fall for the same mistakes ever. Also arrange some homeworks, maybe pass the first week but never pass a week after. short homeworks, taking one or two hours at most enables them to remember more of the context. It's your responsibility to make sure that they remember when they come to the next class.

Well, I don't know which year are you from, but if you are reading here, it's nice to meet you. All the text and the content in this education is 100% human, both me and some dudes in the internet which I validated some info from. Keep it human, love the human, be passionate about coding and whatnot. Always remember to be happy and learn to be resilient. Always feel free to contact me, you know how to find me, you sure know a way. May your way be prosper and your year will be even more successful than our so-called "golden age". Until we meet again!
